02   MATERIALSample and reflection

Δa = surface a − interface a. The normal spacing is assumed to vary linearly. Thickness uses the mean lattice and hkl.

The film scattering amplitude is multiplied by √scale before adding the substrate amplitude. 1 is the reference; 0 removes film scattering. This empirical relative intensity control does not separately measure or distinguish absorption, volume fraction or roughness.

w is an empirical taper width that smoothly reduces the scattering amplitude at both film boundaries. σN describes a discrete thickness distribution across mutually incoherent lateral regions; their intensities are averaged. Both zero retains the original model. Neither directly measures roughness. Compare them one at a time first. Enabling σN increases calculation time.

03   SCANMeasurement and resolution

Mix two unit-area Gaussians as (1 − f)·G(σ) + f·G(mσ). f is the integrated fraction of the broad component (0.005 = 0.5%). Its FWHM is m times the FWHM above. At f = 0, m has no effect; at FWHM = 0, neither setting has an effect.

This model describes a resolution profile; it is not a measured instrument function. Substrate diffraction, mosaicity and other causes can produce similar profiles. A better fit alone does not establish an instrumental origin.

Zero slope gives a constant background. With a slope, the relative background is its value at 2θ = 33° before normalization: b(2θ) = b(33°) × 10^[slope × (2θ − 33°)]. The combined curve is normalized again. A zero relative background makes the slope ineffective.

Vertical and horizontal refer to the scattering plane, assuming a fully horizontally polarized incident beam. Illuminated-length options also multiply by 1/sinθ and assume a fixed incident beam fully contained within the sample. Check your geometry, polarization and beam dimensions. Do not reapply corrections already present in the data.

MEASURED FRINGES

Compare thickness from fringe spacing

Select a measured region with repeating fringes away from the main substrate peak. Uses q = 4π sinθ/λ and thickness = 2π/Δq. Estimated thickness scales with the entered wavelength. This does not change the fitting objective.

Enter maximum angles manually (optional)

Blank angles use automatic detection and assume consecutive orders. Enter at least four increasing manual maxima. Each order corresponds to one maximum; central orders may be skipped. Blank orders mean 0, 1, 2, …. Incorrect orders or unrelated peaks can give the wrong thickness.

DESCRIPTIVE PEAKS

Peak-shape decomposition

Describe the selected range with one or two pseudo-Voigt components and a linear background. A pseudo-Voigt mixes Gaussian and Lorentzian peak shapes. This is not a physical multilayer model and does not change the film simulation above.

Convert using a separate reference peak (optional)

Enter a verified reference peak and its d-spacing. No material or reflection is assumed automatically. This Bragg-ratio conversion assumes a shared zero offset and is shown separately from values based on the entered wavelength.
